# Project Methodology
## INSIDE THE TANK – Startup Investment Analysis Case Study

### 1. Introduction

This document explains the methodology followed to develop the *Inside the Tank – Startup Investment Analysis Case Study*.

The project follows a structured, case-based approach to demonstrate how investors evaluate startups before making funding decisions.

A fictional company, **FreshBite Foods Pvt. Ltd.**, is used to create a realistic business scenario supported by illustrative financial statements, operational data, customer information, and investment proposals.

The methodology combines financial data preparation, Excel-based analysis, business valuation, risk assessment, and classroom simulation.

### 2. Overall Project Approach

The project is divided into the following major phases:

1. Business Case Development
2. Data Preparation and Organisation
3. Financial Statement Development
4. Excel-Based Financial Analysis
5. Business Valuation
6. Investment Evaluation and Risk Assessment
7. Educational Documentation
8. Classroom Investment Simulation

Each phase contributes to the development of a complete startup investment evaluation exercise.

### 3. Phase 1 – Business Case Development

The first phase involves creating the fictional business scenario that forms the foundation of the project.

**Company:** FreshBite Foods Pvt. Ltd.

**Industry:** Food & Beverage

**Business Model:** Manufacturing and selling healthy protein snacks through online platforms, distributors, and retail stores.

The business scenario includes:

- Company background and business operations.
- Product and revenue model.
- Customer and market information.
- Historical financial performance.
- Business expansion requirements.
- Founder investment proposal.

**Investment Proposal:**

| Particulars | Details |
|---|---:|
| Investment Required | ₹50 Lakhs |
| Equity Offered | 5% |
| Post-Money Valuation | ₹10 Crore |
| Pre-Money Valuation | ₹9.5 Crore |

This proposal becomes the starting point for the investment evaluation exercise.

### 4. Phase 2 – Data Preparation and Organisation

The second phase involves developing and organising the datasets required for financial analysis.

The datasets are structured to represent different aspects of the startup's operations.

| Dataset | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Revenue Data | Analyse historical revenue and business growth. |
| Sales Transactions | Evaluate product sales and sales performance. |
| Cost Data | Understand product costs and operating expenses. |
| Income Statement | Evaluate profitability and financial performance. |
| Balance Sheet | Assess assets, liabilities, and financial position. |
| Cash Flow Data | Analyse operating, investing, and financing cash flows. |
| Customer Data | Evaluate customer behaviour and marketing performance. |
| Market Data | Understand market size, growth, and competition. |
| Investment Scenarios | Compare alternative funding and equity proposals. |

**Data Preparation Activities:**

- Organising data into structured tables.
- Maintaining consistent formats and data types.
- Checking missing and duplicate records.
- Validating financial figures and calculations.
- Ensuring consistency between related datasets.
- Documenting assumptions and calculation methods.

All company information and datasets are illustrative and created for educational purposes.

### 5. Phase 3 – Financial Statement Development

Financial statements are developed to provide a foundation for evaluating the company's financial health.

The project includes:

**Income Statement**
- Revenue
- Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
- Gross Profit
- Operating Expenses
- EBITDA
- EBIT
- Profit Before Tax
- Net Profit

**Balance Sheet**
- Current Assets
- Non-Current Assets
- Current Liabilities
- Long-Term Debt
- Shareholders' Equity

**Cash Flow Statement**
- Cash Flow from Operating Activities
- Cash Flow from Investing Activities
- Cash Flow from Financing Activities
- Net Change in Cash

The statements are designed to maintain accounting consistency and support the subsequent analytical models.

### 6. Phase 4 – Excel-Based Financial Analysis

Microsoft Excel is used as the primary tool for financial modelling and business analysis.

The financial evaluation is divided into seven analytical models.

#### Model 01 – Revenue & Business Growth

**Objective:** Evaluate the company's revenue performance and growth trends.

Key activities:
- Calculate annual revenue growth.
- Analyse year-over-year (YoY) growth.
- Evaluate sales volume and average selling price.
- Compare revenue across sales channels.
- Identify growth trends and performance changes.

#### Model 02 – Profitability Analysis

**Objective:** Evaluate the company's ability to generate profits.

Key activities:
- Calculate gross profit and gross profit margin.
- Analyse EBITDA and EBITDA margin.
- Calculate operating expenses as a percentage of revenue.
- Evaluate net profit and net profit margin.
- Compare profitability across financial years.

#### Model 03 – Unit Economics

**Objective:** Understand the profitability of an individual product or transaction.

Key activities:
- Calculate selling price per unit.
- Identify variable costs per unit.
- Calculate contribution per unit.
- Evaluate contribution margin.
- Determine break-even units and sales value.
- Perform cost sensitivity analysis.

#### Model 04 – Customer & Marketing Analysis

**Objective:** Evaluate customer acquisition, retention, and marketing efficiency.

Key activities:
- Calculate Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).
- Evaluate repeat purchase rate.
- Analyse Average Order Value (AOV).
- Estimate Customer Lifetime Value (LTV).
- Calculate the LTV:CAC ratio.
- Evaluate customer and marketing performance.

LTV is treated as an assumption-based estimate where historical customer cohort data is unavailable.

#### Model 05 – Cash Flow & Financial Position

**Objective:** Assess the company's financial stability and cash management.

Key activities:
- Analyse operating, investing, and financing cash flows.
- Calculate current ratio and working capital.
- Evaluate debt-to-equity ratio.
- Analyse receivables, payables, and inventory.
- Examine the company's liquidity and financial position.

#### Model 06 – Business Valuation & Investment

**Objective:** Evaluate the founders' funding proposal and understand the implications of equity investment.

Key activities:
- Calculate pre-money and post-money valuation.
- Evaluate the equity percentage offered.
- Calculate implied valuation multiples.
- Compare alternative investment proposals.
- Analyse ownership dilution.
- Examine different investment scenarios.

#### Model 07 – Investment Evaluation & Risk

**Objective:** Combine financial findings and business risks to support an investment decision.

Key activities:
- Evaluate financial and operational performance.
- Identify business risks and weaknesses.
- Develop a weighted investment evaluation framework.
- Compare investment opportunities.
- Prepare a final investment recommendation.

The weighted evaluation framework is a decision-support tool and does not replace investor judgement or due diligence.

### 7. Phase 5 – Business Valuation

The company's proposed valuation is examined using the investment amount and equity percentage offered by the founders.

The primary calculations are:

**Post-Money Valuation**

Investment Amount ÷ Equity Offered

**Pre-Money Valuation**

Post-Money Valuation − Investment Amount

The project also introduces valuation multiples and alternative investment scenarios to help students understand how funding terms affect company ownership and investor returns.

### 8. Phase 6 – Investment Evaluation and Risk Assessment

After completing the financial models, the findings are consolidated to evaluate the startup's investment potential.

The evaluation considers:

- Revenue growth and sustainability.
- Profitability and operating efficiency.
- Product-level unit economics.
- Customer acquisition and retention.
- Cash flow and liquidity.
- Debt and financial obligations.
- Market opportunity and competition.
- Management and operational risks.
- Valuation and investment terms.

Students use these findings to recommend one of the following outcomes:

1. Accept the founders' proposal.
2. Negotiate revised investment terms.
3. Propose an alternative investment structure.
4. Reject the investment opportunity.

### 9. Phase 7 – Educational Documentation

Supporting educational documents are developed to make the project suitable for classroom learning.

These include:

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Student Case Study | Introduces the business scenario and investment problem. |
| Instructor Guide | Provides teaching instructions and classroom activities. |
| Student Assessment | Evaluates students' understanding and analytical skills. |
| Case Study Solutions | Provides expected calculations, interpretations, and possible investment decisions. |
| Financial Terminology | Explains important financial and investment concepts. |
| Formula Reference | Provides formulas used throughout the Excel analysis. |

### 10. Phase 8 – Shark Tank Classroom Simulation

The final phase converts the financial analysis into an interactive classroom investment exercise.

Students are divided into three groups:

**Founders**
- Present the business model.
- Explain financial performance.
- Justify the proposed valuation.
- Answer investor questions.

**Sharks / Investors**
- Review the financial analysis.
- Identify investment opportunities and risks.
- Negotiate funding and equity terms.
- Make the final investment decision.

**Financial Analysts**
- Verify financial calculations.
- Examine the company's financial position.
- Identify inconsistencies and risks.
- Present analytical findings to support the decision.

The simulation encourages students to apply financial knowledge, business judgement, negotiation, and communication skills.

### 11. Quality Checks and Validation

To maintain the reliability and consistency of the educational case study, the following checks are considered:

- Verify revenue and cost calculations.
- Ensure the income statement is mathematically consistent.
- Confirm that total assets equal total liabilities and equity.
- Reconcile cash flow movements with cash balances.
- Check unit economics and break-even calculations.
- Validate valuation and equity calculations.
- Check relationships between sales, customer, and financial datasets.
- Clearly document assumptions and limitations.

### 12. Final Deliverables

The completed project is organised into five major sections:

1. **Project Documentation:** Project overview, objectives, and methodology.
2. **Excel Financial Analysis:** Financial datasets, calculations, and seven analytical models.
3. **Educational Case Study:** Student and instructor learning materials.
4. **Case Study Solutions:** Expected financial calculations and investment recommendations.
5. **Reference Materials:** Financial terminology, formulas, and supporting sources.
