# Learning Objectives
## INSIDE THE TANK – Startup Investment Analysis Case Study

### 1. Purpose of Learning

The purpose of this case study is to develop students' understanding of how investors evaluate startups before making investment decisions.

Through the fictional business case of **FreshBite Foods Pvt. Ltd.**, students will apply financial concepts, Excel analytical techniques, business evaluation methods, and investment principles to a practical startup scenario.

### 2. Primary Learning Objectives

By completing this project, students should be able to:

1. Understand the fundamentals of startup funding, equity investment, and business ownership.
2. Interpret financial statements from an investor's perspective.
3. Evaluate business growth using revenue trends, growth rates, and sales performance.
4. Analyse profitability through gross profit, EBITDA, net profit, and financial margins.
5. Understand unit economics, contribution margins, and break-even analysis.
6. Calculate and interpret customer acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (LTV), and repeat purchase rates.
7. Evaluate a company's cash flow, liquidity, working capital, and debt position.
8. Understand startup valuation, pre-money valuation, post-money valuation, and equity dilution.
9. Identify financial, operational, and market-related business risks.
10. Compare investment scenarios and assess their potential financial implications.
11. Develop data-driven investment recommendations.
12. Communicate and defend investment decisions using financial evidence.

### 3. Technical Learning Objectives

Students will develop practical skills in the following areas:

| Area | Skills to Develop |
|---|---|
| Excel Fundamentals | Data organisation, formulas, calculations, and financial modelling |
| Financial Analysis | Profitability, liquidity, leverage, and efficiency ratios |
| Data Analysis | Trend analysis, growth calculations, and performance comparisons |
| Statistical Analysis | Mean, median, standard deviation, and correlation |
| Unit Economics | Contribution analysis and break-even calculations |
| Valuation | Revenue multiples, P/E ratios, and equity valuation |
| Scenario Analysis | Sensitivity analysis and alternative investment scenarios |
| Decision Modelling | Weighted evaluation and investment scoring |

### 4. Investor Thinking & Business Evaluation

Students should learn to think beyond financial calculations and evaluate a startup from an investor's perspective.

They should be able to:

- Determine whether a startup's growth is sustainable.
- Assess whether the business generates sufficient profit from its operations.
- Understand whether its products have attractive unit economics.
- Evaluate customer retention and acquisition efficiency.
- Identify potential cash flow and funding requirements.
- Examine whether the founder's valuation is reasonable.
- Recognise business risks that could affect future performance.
- Decide whether the expected business opportunity justifies the investment risk.

### 5. Practical Learning Through Classroom Simulation

Through the **Shark Tank Classroom Challenge**, students will apply their learning in a simulated investment environment.

| Role | Learning Objective |
|---|---|
| Founders | Present the business, justify financial performance, and defend the proposed valuation. |
| Sharks / Investors | Evaluate the opportunity, negotiate equity terms, and make investment decisions. |
| Financial Analysts | Verify calculations, identify risks, and present evidence-based findings. |

### 6. Expected Learning Outcomes

At the end of the case study, students should be able to:

- Perform a structured financial evaluation of a startup.
- Build and interpret financial models using Excel.
- Understand the relationship between business performance and valuation.
- Assess investment proposals and negotiate equity terms.
- Identify potential risks and opportunities.
- Prepare a clear investment recommendation supported by financial analysis.
- Present and defend their decisions in a professional business environment.

### 7. Target Audience

This case study is suitable for:

- B.Com students
- BBA students
- MBA students
- Finance and accounting students
- Entrepreneurship students
- Beginners interested in financial modelling and startup investment analysis

**Recommended Difficulty Level:** Beginner to Intermediate

### 8. Final Learning Goal

The ultimate goal is to help students transition from simply understanding financial formulas to thinking like financial analysts and investors.

**Students should learn not just how to calculate a company's financial performance, but how to use that information to decide whether a business is worth investing in.**
